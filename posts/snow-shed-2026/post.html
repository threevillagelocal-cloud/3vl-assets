The shop on 112

Where Long Island winters started

Chances are, if you skied or rode anywhere around Setauket, Stony Brook, Port Jefferson or Terryville over the last four decades, your season started at Snow Shed. You walked in with a kid who had grown two boot sizes since April, walked out with a season lease, a fresh wax and a lift ticket tucked in your pocket, and pointed the car north.

📍 1004 Route 112, Port Jefferson Station📅 Opened 1982❄️ Closed 2026, after 44 years

Snow Shed sold and rented skis, snowboards and cross-country gear, plus bindings, boots, winter outerwear and thermal layers. Behind the counter was a full tune shop: tune-ups, hot waxes, stone grinds, base repairs and binding mounts.

Its logo said it simply: a mountain range, Snow Shed, Long Island, NY and Since 1982. This year, owner Chad signed the shop's farewell note to its customers.

Inside the shop

Getting fitted

The fall ritual: leases, boots and a fresh tune

The real heart of Snow Shed was the seasonal lease. Every fall, families came in for skis, boots and poles sized for the winter ahead, then brought them back in spring. The shop's old website promised that leases and daily rentals came pre-tuned, waxed and adjusted, and added: “We take the time and effort to fit each person up properly.”

There was a sweetener for families, too. On the winter 2010-11 rental page, kids 12 and under got a free lift ticket to Jiminy Peak with any seasonal lease. Years later, the site still listed Jiminy Peak as Snow Shed's affiliate mountain.

The tune shop never went out of style. On the shop's 2023-24 service menu, a full tune-up (flat filed, edges sharpened, base hot waxed) was $34.99, right next to boot heat molding, base repair and binding adjustments.

The gear we all remember

From neon one-pieces to the snowboard wall

Snow Shed opened in 1982, right as skiing was at its most colorful: long straight skis, rear-entry boots, and one-piece suits in colors that could be seen from the summit. Over the next 44 years the shop watched the whole sport change. Snowboards went from a curiosity to a fixture on the sales floor, and skis got shorter, wider and shaped so a beginner could carve a turn on day one.

Snowboarding had to fight its way onto the mountain. In the 1980s, Stratton in southern Vermont became the first ski area to allow snowboarding, and other mountains followed over time. By the 2010s, Snow Shed's website showed Burton, Ride, GNU and Lib Tech boards right alongside the skis.

The road trip

Skis on the roof, pointed north

Long Island has plenty going for it, but mountains aren't on the list. So a Snow Shed season always ended up on the road: off the Island before dawn, skis on the roof, a thermos in the cup holder, and a car full of people asking how much longer. Snow Shed even sold the roof racks, carrying Thule car rack systems for years.

The gear got you there. The memories were made at the top of the lift: first runs, first falls, hot chocolate in the lodge, and the long, happy, tired ride home.

Plot twist

Snow Shed had a summer side

A lot of people forget this part. Around 2009 and 2010, the shop's website called itself Snow Shed Ski and Marine. Next to the skis it listed wakeboards, water skis, tubes, life vests, wetsuits and boogie boards, plus Hobie, Old Town and Ocean kayaks and stand-up paddle boards. In 2009 it even listed a separate marine location with boats.

So for a while, the same shop that sent you to Vermont in January sent you out on the Sound in July.

The goodbye

A shop that took care of its people to the end

In June 2026, Snow Shed told customers it had been fighting to keep the shop going for five years and had run out of options. Customers had until June 19 to pick up skis left for tune-ups and repairs, and the closing sale took 40 to 70 percent off. By the end of June, Greater Long Island reported a for-sale sign out front.

What stands out is how Snow Shed said goodbye. Instead of just locking the door, owner Chad picked one shop to send his customers to: Sno-Haus in Huntington Station, a family-owned Long Island ski shop since 1959. In his note, he wrote that he didn't want to hand people off somewhere “nobody knows your kid's boot size or what you skied last season.”

Snow Shed seasonal renters from 2025-26 were offered a free basic 2026-27 seasonal rental at Sno-Haus Huntington Station (terms apply), and Snow Shed rental gear could be returned there through October 15, 2026. If you still have questions, Sno-Haus Huntington Station is at 2 W Jericho Turnpike.

That's the kind of thing a neighborhood shop does. “We will certainly miss all of you,” the shop wrote. Three Village will miss you too, Snow Shed.
